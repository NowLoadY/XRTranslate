# Example briefs

Adapt only the details that serve the user's request. Tool fields such as size, filename, quality and transparency sit outside the prompt.

## Documentary establishing shot

Use case: photorealistic-natural

Intended use: wide establishing still for a walking documentary.

Prompt: “A quiet river footpath on an overcast morning, seen from walking height. Fine gravel shows through the damp earth, with soft reflections along the water's edge. Natural light, subdued color and clear depth along the path. Keep the lower third calm for the project's caption.”

## Product, game or story illustration

Use case: stylized-concept

Prompt: “A small blue ceramic lantern, front three-quarter view. Painted storybook illustration with visible gouache brushwork and a clear handle silhouette. Keep the whole lantern in frame with room around its edges.”

For an overlay, request transparency in the tool. For related shots, use the approved lantern as a reference and state which details continue from it.

## Diagram or interface

Use case: infographic-diagram

Prompt: “A landscape classroom card showing three stages of the water cycle in reading order. A simple landscape connects the labeled stages ‘Evaporation’, ‘Condensation’ and ‘Precipitation’. Use restrained colors, distinct directional arrows and lettering large enough to read on a projector. Render each label verbatim once.”

## Local object edit

Use case: precise-object-edit

Prompt: “Image 1 is the edit target. Change the blue lantern's glaze to amber. Preserve its silhouette, handle, painted highlights, framing and background.”

## Composite with a reference

Use case: compositing

Prompt: “Image 1 is the base desk scene. Image 2 supplies the ceramic lantern. Put the lantern beside the notebook on the desk, respecting the desk's perspective and the existing window light. Preserve the notebook, furniture and camera position.”

## In-image copy

Use case: text-localization

Prompt: “Image 1 is the original station sign. Replace ‘EAST GATE’ with ‘东门’. Preserve the sign's perspective, frame, surface wear, letter spacing and lighting. Keep all surrounding features in place.”
